    Input: shorter, denser sentences

    A participle phrase packs a whole idea into a few words. It replaces a clause, so your sentence is shorter and sounds more academic.

    Why it matters

    Band 6 writers use because, so and and this again and again. Band 7+ writers sometimes replace them with a participle phrase.

    Band 6Many young people live in big cities and they feel lonely.
    →
    Band 7+Living in big cities, many young people feel lonely.

    Reference table: five structures

    the participle phrasethe main clause

    The “same subject” rule

    The participle phrase has no subject of its own. It borrows the subject of the main clause. Ask: who does the action?

    Not thisHaving finished the exam, the results were disappointing.
    →
    ThisHaving finished the exam, the students found the results disappointing.

    In the first sentence it sounds as if the results finished the exam. Examiners call this a “dangling participle”. It is the most common mistake with this structure.
