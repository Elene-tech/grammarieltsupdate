    The flip: fact → imagined fact

    Type 2, Type 3 and mixed conditionals describe things that are not true. So you must flip the real fact: positive becomes negative, negative becomes positive.

    Real factBuses are expensive, so many people drive.
    →
    ImaginedIf buses were cheaper, fewer people would drive.

    Remember: the real fact has no “not”, the imagined result has one (fewer / not).

    The upgrade ladder

    Plain · Band 6Many people drive because buses are too expensive.
    Conditional · Band 7If buses were cheaper, fewer people would drive.
    Formal · Band 7.5Were buses to become cheaper, fewer people would drive.
    1. Find the cause and the result.
    2. Choose the time: past, present or future.
    3. Flip the fact.
    4. Optional: make it formal with Were … to, Had or Should.

      Traffic congestion is a growing problem in many large cities. One solution is to improve public transport. AIf city councils lowered ticket prices, more commuters would leave their cars at home. BProvided that trains and buses are frequent and reliable, this change could reduce traffic quickly. In addition, congestion charges should be introduced in city centres. CUnless drivers pay for using busy roads, they will have little reason to change their habits. AIf more companies allowed remote work, workers would spend less on transport and more time with their families. However, there are drawbacks. BWere remote work to become the norm, many employees would feel isolated and find it harder to learn from colleagues.
