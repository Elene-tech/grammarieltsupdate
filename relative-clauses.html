    8–22 min

    Input: add detail without new sentences

    A relative clause adds information to a noun. It lets you build one rich sentence instead of three short ones.

    Why it matters

    Band 6 essays use many short sentences. Band 7+ essays pack in more information with who, which, whose and , which.

    Band 6Some students sleep too little. They often perform badly.
    →
    Band 7+Students who sleep too little often perform badly.

    Reference table: six structures

    the noun you describethe relative clause

    Defining or non-defining?

    Defining clauses tell us which people or things. There are no commas. Non-defining clauses add extra information. They have commas, and you can delete them without changing the main message.

    Defining (no commas)Students who sleep badly often fail exams.
    →
    Non-defining (commas)Tokyo, which has 14 million people, has an excellent metro.

    Test: delete the clause. “Students often fail exams” changes the meaning, so it is defining. “Tokyo has an excellent metro” still works, so it is non-defining.
