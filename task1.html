📊 Task 1 Lab — reports, graphs and processes

The same grammar that wins marks in Task 2 works differently in a report. Here you will learn to describe trends with participles, write overviews with nominalisation, compare data precisely, and handle process diagrams with the passive.

🗺️What Task 1 needs

Task 1 is a report, not an argument: no opinions, no “I believe”. Your grammar score comes from describing data accurately and grouping it clearly.

Line / bar / pie charts (changes & comparisons)
participles · comparatives · nominalisation
Process diagrams (how something is made)
passives · sequencers
Maps (how a place changed)
passives · there is / there was
The overview (the most important paragraph!)
nominalisation · overall, …

🧰The five tools

1 · Participles for trends

verb + adverb, -ing + noun / number
“The price of petrol rose sharply between 2000 and 2010, peaking at $1.60 per litre.”
“Car use fell steadily, ending the period at just 20%.”
Watch out: the participle must describe the same subject. “The price rose, peaking at…” ✓ — the price peaks, not the writer.

2 · Nominalisation for overviews

there was a + adjective + noun + in …
“Overall, there was a significant increase in car ownership, while bus use saw a steady decline.”
“The most dramatic change was the growth of online shopping.”
Watch out: one idea per noun phrase. “a sharp rise in prices” ✓ — not “a sharp rise in prices was happened”.

3 · Precise comparison

twice / three times as … as · far / marginally + comparative
“In 2010, twice as many people travelled by train as by bus.”
“Spending on books was marginally higher than spending on magazines.”
Watch out: “more higher” ✗ — never double the comparative.

4 · Passives for processes

is / are + past participle · is then + past participle
“First, the beans are harvested and dried in the sun. They are then roasted at high temperatures.”
Watch out: in a process we usually do not know or care WHO does it — that is why the passive fits. Keep it; do not invent “workers” or “people”.

5 · Sequencers that sound academic

initially → subsequently → at the next stage → finally
“Initially, the mixture is heated. Subsequently, it is cooled and filtered. At the final stage, the product is packaged.”
Watch out: vary your sequencers — “then … then … then” reads as Band 5.

🏆Final task — write the overview

The overview is the highest-value paragraph in Task 1. Without one, Task Achievement is capped at Band 5.

The chart (imagined): In 2000, 40% of households had internet access; by 2020 the figure was 92%. Over the same period, landline telephones fell from 95% of households to 30%. Mobile phones rose from 20% to 97%.

Write a 2-sentence overview (main trends only — no numbers!). Use nominalisation: there was a … rise / decline in …

0 words · target 30–45
Model · Band 7.5 Overall, there was a dramatic rise in internet access and mobile phone ownership, while landline use saw a steep decline. By the end of the period, mobile phones and the internet had become almost universal, with both technologies present in over nine out of ten households.
Note: two trends named, no data in the first sentence, one precise figure only in support. Nominalisation does the heavy lifting.
