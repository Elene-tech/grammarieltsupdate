    Input: turn actions into things

    Nominalisation means turning a verb or an adjective into a noun. The noun phrase can then be the subject of your sentence. This is how academic English sounds.

    Why it matters

    Band 6 writers describe people doing things. Band 7+ writers describe ideas and trends. They write about “the rise in pollution”, not “people pollute more”.

    Band 6Many people are unemployed, so young people suffer and cities have more crime.
    →
    Band 7+Youth unemployment contributes to a rise in crime.

    Reference table: four ways to use nominalisation

    the idea you turn into a nounthe rest of the sentence

    Same meaning, different focus

    A nominalised sentence is shorter, and the idea becomes the subject.

    Verb styleGovernments reduced taxes, and so people spent more.
    →
    Noun styleA reduction in taxes led to an increase in spending.

    Do not nominalise every sentence. Too many abstract nouns make writing hard to read. Use it for your main ideas, and keep some simple verbs.
